How the ground compares with the real places

Six in-game captures from four areas, each set beside an openly licensed photo of the same road or valley, with colour statistics measured on the ground band. Below are the gaps, ranked by how much they hurt the picture, each with 1–3 fix options. No game code or content was changed.

Short version: far and mid hills already read correctly from the satellite colour. The trouble is the first 100 m. Every area gets the same olive-tan ground. Ground cover reads as evenly spaced dots, not a surface. The road edge is one mossy strip everywhere, even under snow. Slopes have no erosion detail.

Measurements

Ground band = the lower 45% of the frame minus its middle third, which drops the car and HUD. Luma is mean linear luminance; saturation is the median HSV S; hue is the circular mean over saturated pixels; detail is the median 16 px block standard deviation of luma, divided by the mean.

  • The hue never moves. Game ground sits at 41–46° (olive-tan) in desert, alpine meadow, tropical coast and Cascade pasture alike. The references span 28° (Green Valley dirt) to 75° (Skagit pasture).
  • Saturation runs the wrong way. Dry places come out 2–4× too saturated (Green Valley 0.43 vs 0.10). Green places come out half as saturated as they should and too yellow (Skagit 0.23 vs 0.43).
  • Detail is lower at five of six spots (0.16–0.22 vs 0.19–0.73), and the deficit is largest where real ground is busiest (Oahu, Green Valley).
  • Source albedo, measured on the ambientCG colour maps: Grass001 S = 0.55 and Ground037 S = 0.44, which is already more saturated than any real ground band here. Snow006 linear luma is 0.58; real fresh snow is 0.8–0.9. The per-area Sentinel-2 albedo has S = 0.10–0.31, close to the photos, but it gets only 25% weight near the camera.

Gaps and fix options

1

One ground colour for every place

Waimanalo, a lawn-and-red-dirt beach park under ironwoods, renders as grey-beige gravel. Concrete WA, a wet green pasture valley, renders as a dry brown field. Agua Dulce gets a strong ochre where the real dirt is pale and dusty. The near ground is mostly the ambientCG layer tinted by the biome colour (TINT_KEEP 0.35). The satellite colour, the only per-place colour source, gets just 25% weight until 200 m out. Its texels are 20 m.

A. Let the satellite colour set hue and value near the camera recommended first

Raise the sat_macro near weight from 25% to about 60%. Then use the ambientCG layers only for detail: luminance-normalise each layer and multiply by the satellite albedo, rather than tinting the layer. Every area then inherits its own measured colour.

gain: highGPU ~0 ms (same samples)effort S: apply_terrain_real_materials.pylicence: Sentinel-2 open data, already used
B. Recalibrate the texture albedo to measured ranges

Clamp each layer's mean S and luma to photo-measured targets (grass S≈0.30, dry dirt S≈0.15, snow luma ≥0.8) at import time. Keep a small per-biome palette taken from Sentinel-2 class means, so cured grass in Green Valley and lush grass at Oahu stop sharing one colour.

gain: medium-highGPU 0 mseffort Slicence: existing CC0 textures
C. 10 m Sentinel-2 bands instead of 20 m

B2/B3/B4 are native 10 m: four times the texels, so fields, dirt lots and gravel pads keep their shape at 50–200 m.

gain: medium (mid range)GPU ~0 ms, +~30–60 MB VRAM per areaeffort S: build_sat_albedo.pylicence: Copernicus open
2

Ground cover reads as dots, not cover

Real verges and fields are a continuous, mixed surface: short grass, cured stems, forbs and litter, with shrubs in clumps. The game puts identical tufts at even spacing on bare ground, which gives a polka-dot look. At Dillon the shrubs form a visible grid. At Concrete there is almost nothing: the log shows 1 cell built with 0 grass. Cards stop at 72–120 m, and nothing fills in beyond that.

A. Make grass a ground layer, with cards as accents recommended

Paint the base layer as dense short grass (or cured grass, per biome) using a grass albedo plus a fine normal, with height-blended bare patches driven by noise and the satellite colour. Then use cards only for tall clumps. This removes the bare-dirt-between-tufts look at every distance, including past 120 m.

gain: highGPU ≤0.1 ms (one more layer sample)effort Mlicence: ambientCG CC0 grass sets, or procedural
B. Clumped, mixed placement

Replace lattice and uniform placement with jittered placement weighted by a low-frequency clump noise. Mix 2–3 scales and a cured/green colour variant per instance, and pick species per biome. The instance count stays the same.

gain: medium-highGPU 0 ms (same count)effort S-M: ODGroundCover.cpplicence: n/a
C. Fix the empty spots

Trace why Concrete builds 1 cell with 0 grass; the town and field mask is the likely cause. Cover there should match the ditch and field edges.

gain: medium (local)GPU smalleffort Slicence: n/a
3

The road edge is one mossy strip everywhere

All six spots show the same olive-moss band of about 1.5 m, in desert, alpine, coastal and Cascade settings, and under snow. Real roads here have a pale gravel or dust shoulder 1–3 m wide (CO 9 reference), then a foreslope and ditch, then the verge. In winter they have ploughed banks. The shoulder texture Gravel040 has linear luma 0.128, darker than most of the surrounding ground. No ditch geometry exists anywhere; embankment skirts only cover drops of 2.5–7 m.

A. Retune the shoulder layer recommended first

Swap in a paler CC0 gravel (target luma ~0.25–0.30, like Gravel030), widen it to 1.5–2.5 m with a noisy edge, choose dust or gravel by climate, and let snow cover it in winter.

gain: medium-highGPU 0 mseffort Slicence: ambientCG CC0
B. Verge strip mesh with a ditch profile

Build a road-parallel strip (shoulder, foreslope, ditch, backslope) the way build_road_embankments.py builds skirts. It can also carry ploughed snowbanks in winter.

gain: highGPU ~0.1–0.3 mseffort Mlicence: n/a
C. RVT road-edge blend (planned Join J)

Write the roads into a runtime virtual texture so terrain blends into asphalt with dirt and tyre tracks.

gain: mediumGPU ~0.3–0.5 mseffort M-Hlicence: n/a
4

Slopes lack erosion detail and show facets

The real San Gabriel foothills behind Agua Dulce are cut with gullies and pale bare rock. In the game the same hills are smooth with broad colour changes. Near slopes show large flat triangles, because the RTIN tiles carry per-vertex normals only. The terrain material binds no normal or detail map from the DEM.

A. A DEM normal and curvature texture per area recommended

Bake a normal plus curvature map from USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar (public domain; the US areas) or the current DEM elsewhere, using the same world-XY projection as SatTex. The normals restore gullies and ridges at every range. Curvature drives rock and dirt into gullies and grass onto ridges.

gain: highGPU ~0.1 ms (1–2 samples)effort M (pipeline + material)licence: USGS public domain
B. Denser near-tile mesh

Lower martini error near the road, or use more edge loops on cut slopes, to cut the facets in silhouette.

gain: low-mediumGPU ~0.2 ms (triangles)effort Slicence: n/a
C. Nanite terrain with displacement

Rebuild the terrain as Nanite static meshes. Best silhouettes, but a large rewrite and real GPU cost on this laptop.

gain: highGPU 1–3 mseffort Hnot recommended now
5

Snow is a flat sheet

In winter the ground is an even grey-white with no patches, drifts or bare south faces. Rocks and the shoulder stay bare, and the grass cards turn bright white and pop out. The Frisco reference (early winter) shows straw grass with snow patchy by aspect. Snow006 is 0.58 linear luma against 0.8–0.9 for fresh snow. The lighting session also logged white-twig snow on foliage while the ground stayed bare under the saved Winter season.

A. Snow albedo plus patchiness recommended

Lift snow to about 0.8 albedo. Drive cover by slope, aspect (south faces melt), DEM curvature (drifts in hollows) and noise, so partial seasons look like Frisco in December.

gain: medium-highGPU 0 mseffort Slicence: existing CC0
B. Snow on top of rocks, shoulder and cards

Add a world-normal-Z snow mask in the rock and ground-cover materials, tied to the same season parameter, and dim card albedo in snow.

gain: mediumGPU ≤0.05 mseffort Slicence: n/a
6

Screen-door pattern on mid-range ground

A fine regular dot pattern covers the field at Dillon and the open ground at Oahu, at 15–60 m. It may be the dithered fade of the grass carpet, which spans 60–120 m, or high-frequency texture aliasing from the anti-tiling copy (×3.7). The cause is not yet confirmed.

A. Isolate, then fix

Capture once with the grass carpet off, then once with the far texture copy off. Then either soften the dither (wider fade, TSR-friendly noise) or add mip bias and texture bombing.

gain: mediumGPU 0 mseffort Slicence: n/a
Outside this scope, noted for the forest and distance-LOD owners: at Skagit the forested hills render as scattered conifers on bright grass, where the real valley sides are continuous dark canopy. Far hills elsewhere (Dillon, Green Valley) already read well from the satellite colour.

Capture settings

All shots used the real -game path on the GPU, launched from the main checkout's OpenDriveUE.uproject at commit fdc7f2c. Settings as played: sp 100 sg 3 style 1 (from the ODPerf line), Asset Detail Standard (0, from the saved GameUserSettings), Lumen, VSM, _real materials, forest, far-forest cards, ground cover, buildings and traffic all on. The window ran at 1280×720 (-resx=1920 -resy=1080 was requested). Each screenshot fired at -odshot=480 s, well after the loading gate released.
