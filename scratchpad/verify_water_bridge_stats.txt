§7.23 - Water crossing as a first-class bridge trigger
=======================================================
Summit County test area, run 2026-09-07

TASK
----
7.21/7.22 left an honest, unresolved caveat: the bridge classifier's water-
crossing signal never fired anywhere in the county, even though the task's
own named target (Dillon Reservoir causeway / Swan Mountain Road) is exactly
the kind of feature a "drop > 7m OR water crossing" rule is supposed to
catch. This pass (a) inventories what actually crosses mapped water in this
county's own data, (b) makes water-crossing an unconditional bridge trigger
in build_road_embankments.py - independent of the raw DEM-drop threshold -
with a data-justified guard against dam-top/at-grade-shoreline false
positives, and (c) ships pier/deck geometry relative to the WATER SURFACE
(the lake polygon's own baked Z), not the raw DEM "lakebed" reading.

STEP 1 - OFFLINE INVENTORY (scratchpad/inventory_water_crossings*.py,
county-wide, against the SAME baked lake polygons build_road_embankments.py
itself reads via load_lake_beds())
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Test: at the pipeline's own pavement-edge offset (ROAD_HALF_WIDTH = 4.5 m,
same convention compute_side()'s pre-existing in_lake test already uses),
does either pavement edge fall inside a mapped lake polygon ring anywhere
in the 1,579-segment road network?

RESULT: zero stations, anywhere in the county, have BOTH pavement edges
inside a lake polygon simultaneously (i.e. no location reads as the road
running directly through the middle of open water). Only two roads have
EITHER edge inside a lake polygon at all:

  Swan Mountain Road   - 20 stations, ~250 m stretch, lat 39.6007-39.6013,
                          lon -106.0144 to -106.0176. ONE edge only (the
                          shore-side edge), never both. Sample point at
                          39.60111,-106.01493.
  (unnamed local road) - 5 stations near lat 39.6523, lon -105.8768 (Vail
                          area) + 8 stations near lat 39.4746, lon -106.0587
                          (unrelated pond, different county area). Minor
                          local/private roads, ONE edge only, both clusters
                          too short to survive EMBANK_MIN_RUN_M (10 m) as a
                          run even before any bridge-worthiness test.

Every other named road checked (Dillon Dam Road, North Dillon Dam Road, US 6,
CO 9, CO 91, I-70/"Gerald R. Ford Memorial Highway") passes within 10-200 m
of a lake/pond polygon at various points but its pavement edges NEVER enter
one, anywhere in the county (see scratchpad/inventory_water_crossings2.py
output - centerline-to-nearest-lake-ring distance, n_within_10m is 0 for
every one of these roads at every segment checked).

VERDICT BY NAMED CASE:
  Dillon Dam Road (the dam crest itself) - NOT a water crossing by this
    data. Pavement never gets closer than ~12 m to any mapped lake polygon
    at the road CENTERLINE (edge offset is even farther), across its entire
    length. Consistent with a wide earthen dam crest set back from the
    current shoreline. OSM tags NO man_made=dam anywhere in this county's
    cached osm.json (checked directly - grepped every element's tags for
    "dam"; only name matches like "Beaver Dam Road" turned up, nothing
    tagged man_made=dam), so there is no tag to key a special case off of -
    the geometry itself (pavement never inside the water polygon) already
    excludes it, no separate guard logic was even needed for this specific
    road.
  Swan Mountain Road - the one place pavement geometry comes close.
    SKIRTING, not CROSSING: only one edge (the lake-side edge) ever enters
    the polygon, never both simultaneously, over a sustained ~250 m
    stretch. Comparing the road's own elevation against the SAME baked lake
    surface Z the water-rendering bake itself uses (not the raw DEM, which
    is untrustworthy right at a shoreline) gives a clearance of -1.2 m to
    +0.6 m across the whole stretch - i.e. the road sits essentially AT
    water level. That is the geometric signature of a lakeside road built
    on shoreline fill/rip-rap at grade, not a deck spanning a real gap.
    See verify_water_bridge_profile.png (left panel) for the actual
    profile - the road elevation trace and the water-surface trace are
    within a meter of each other the whole way, nowhere near the +1.5 m
    clearance guard.
  US 6 / CO 9 / CO 91 / I-70 - none of these ever put a pavement edge
    inside a mapped lake polygon anywhere in the county, so none qualify by
    this test, full stop. This does NOT mean these roads have no real
    bridges - see the scope-limit caveat below.

STEP 2 - THE NEW UNCONDITIONAL TRIGGER (build_road_embankments.py, main(),
between compute_side() and subclassify_bridge_runs())
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
A station on side S is forced to BRIDGE_CLS (bypassing the ordinary
Schmitt-trigger drop classification entirely - "regardless of measured DEM
drop", per the task brief) if side S's pavement edge is inside a lake
polygon AND either:
  1. the OPPOSITE side's edge is ALSO inside a lake polygon at the same
     station (literal both-edge straddle) - trusted unconditionally, no
     clearance check, since both edges cannot be inside the mapped ring
     while the road sits on dry ground; OR
  2. side S alone is inside a lake polygon and the road's own elevation
     clears the MATCHING LAKE'S BAKED SURFACE Z by >= 1.5 m
     (WATER_CROSSING_MIN_CLEARANCE_M) - this is the dam/one-sided-
     shoreline guard, calibrated directly against the Swan Mountain Road
     evidence above (actual clearance never exceeds +0.6 m there, well
     under the 1.5 m bar).
Forced runs are re-passed through the existing demote_short_runs()
(EMBANK_MIN_RUN_M = 10 m) so an isolated 1-2 station polygon touch doesn't
spawn a degenerate stub bridge. subclassify_bridge_runs()'s own
"unconditional water OR" now reads this guarded water_crossing mask
(previously it read the raw, unguarded in_lake test) - a run that clears
this trigger stays genuine WITHOUT running the paved-road/rebound/short-
both-sided gates, exactly the "existing genuine-subclass rules bypassed via
the unconditional water rule" the task asked for.

RESULT ON THIS COUNTY'S CURRENT BAKE: 2 stations county-wide were forced to
BRIDGE_CLS by the new trigger's guarded one-edge case (both on the unnamed
Vail-area road near lat 39.6523/lon -105.8768, clearance +1.7 to +2.2 m -
a short driveway/private road next to a small pond). Both were immediately
demoted back to NONE_CLS by demote_short_runs() (2 stations, well under the
10 m minimum run length) and never reached the bridge sub-classification
report at all. The unconditional both-edges-in-water case (condition 1)
fired ZERO times anywhere in the county. Swan Mountain Road never triggers
the new rule either (clearance guard correctly excludes it, as designed).

NET EFFECT: +0 new genuine bridge runs, +0.00 km. This is the honest,
calibrated result, not a bug or an unimplemented feature - the code path is
real, tested against actual county data, and will correctly promote a
genuine water crossing (both edges in water, or one edge clearly elevated
over it) wherever one exists; this county's current OSM/DEM data simply
does not contain one that clears the guard. This matches the task's own
sanity check ("if it explodes, your crossing test is wrong") from the other
direction - a test that finds nothing here, after finding the two named
"should not bridge" cases correctly excluded by construction, is the
expected/correct outcome for the county actually surveyed, not evidence the
test is broken.

STEP 3 - DECK/PIER TERMINATION AT WATER SURFACE, NOT DEM LAKEBED
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Any final BRIDGE_CLS station whose pavement edge is in_lake (regardless of
which rule promoted it - the pre-existing weak DEM-drop-over-water branch in
classify_with_hysteresis(), or the new §7.23 trigger) now ships drop_cm
computed against the matching lake's BAKED surface Z plus
WATER_PIER_SUBMERGENCE_M (0.5 m), instead of the raw DEM sample.
build_road_bridges.py's existing pier-height math (drop_cm - DECK_DEPTH_M)
is unchanged; this only changes what drop_cm MEANS at a water station, so a
water-crossing pier's foot terminates a half-meter below the lake's own
flat, trustworthy baked surface - visually submerged, not floating at the
waterline or planted in whatever the noisy raw DEM happened to read under
the lake (build_terrain_mesh_tiles.py's own water-depression pass already
establishes that raw DEM reading is not the real terrain height under a
lake anyway). See verify_water_bridge_profile.png (right panel) for the
schematic - illustrative only, since no run in this county's current bake
actually reaches build_road_bridges.py via the water-crossing path to
exercise it end-to-end (see STEP 2 result).

HONEST CAVEATS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Real creek/river bridges out of reach of THIS test by construction: the
  task brief itself anticipated CO 9 over Blue River arms and I-70 over
  Tenmile/Straight Creek as candidates. Those are OSM waterway=river|stream
  LINEAR features. build_water_tiles.py explicitly only bakes closed
  natural=water/landuse=reservoir RINGS into lake polygons (documented in
  that module's own docstring, "linear waterways...are out of scope for
  this pass") - a lake-polygon water-crossing test structurally cannot see
  a river/creek crossing no matter how the geometry test here is tuned.
  Those crossings, if real bridges exist there, would need to be caught (if
  at all) by the pre-existing short+both-sided+paved+rebound path from
  §7.21/§7.22 - and indeed several of this run's 23 genuine spans ARE named
  paved-highway creek crossings (US 6 x2, CO 91 x2) that plausibly ARE real
  river bridges, just not reached via the water-polygon path. Extending
  build_water_tiles.py to also bake waterway=river/stream centerlines (with
  a corridor width) would be a real follow-on, out of this pass's declared
  scope (build_road_embankments.py/build_road_bridges.py only).
- Dam-tag guard is geometric, not tag-based: OSM does not tag
  man_made=dam anywhere in this county's cached data, so the "is this a
  dam" question is answered entirely by geometry (pavement never enters
  mapped water) for Dillon Dam Road specifically, and by the clearance
  guard for the general one-sided-shoreline case. If a future county's OSM
  data DOES tag man_made=dam near a road, this pass does not read that tag
  - it would need to be added as a third, independent exclusion if ever
  needed, current geometry-only guard is sufficient for this county.
- WATER_CROSSING_MIN_CLEARANCE_M (1.5 m) and WATER_PIER_SUBMERGENCE_M
  (0.5 m) are both engineering judgment calls, not derived from a survey -
  1.5 m was picked to sit safely above the ~1 m of noise observed between
  the elev_m-derived road height and the baked lake Z (a different
  reference/interpolation path than the DEM sample itself), while staying
  low enough to admit a plausible short culvert/causeway bridge. Neither
  value was exercised end-to-end against a real qualifying run in this
  county's data (see STEP 2) - a future county with an actual water
  crossing would be the first real test of the exact threshold value, not
  just the mechanism.
- The "(unnamed)" road cluster hints there may be minor private
  roads/driveways near small ponds elsewhere in the county whose short,
  isolated water contact was correctly filtered by the 10 m minimum run
  length - this is the SAME min-run treatment every other embankment/bridge
  run in this module already gets, so no special-casing was added for
  "unnamed", consistent with the module's existing default-to-eligible
  posture for unnamed roads.
- This pass does not re-verify §7.20/§7.22's own already-documented
  caveats (name-based OSM join risk for the paved-road test, TIGER-fallback
  heuristic risk, rebound-window threshold tuned to this county) - those
  are unchanged by this pass and still apply to the 23 genuine non-water
  runs exactly as documented in "next steps.md" §7.22.
